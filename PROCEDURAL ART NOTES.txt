PROCEDURAL ART FOR THE VISUALIZER -- what's built, how to play with it, what to add next
====================================================================================

WHAT CHANGED
  The music now picks the visual. With the gear playing (and no Push pad touched for
  20 seconds), the hub moves to the next visual every phrase (8 bars, desk > Audio >
  "Auto: new pattern every") and on every drop:
     flower > warp > golden > truchet > metatron > quasicrystal > contours > yantra >
     life > attractor > chladni > mandala > harmonograph > tesseract > voronoi >
     reaction > kaleido > ...      (a new one every 2 bars by default)
  Breakdowns switch to the slow, organic ones (warp, contours, reaction, attractor);
  right after a drop it shows everything blended. Touch a pad and that pad's geometry
  takes over for 20 seconds, as before. Every bar the picture also turns 45 degrees,
  and each phrase gets a new size / line weight.
  Audio reactive everywhere: the kick pumps the size, the loudness drives the glow,
  the snare lifts it, the hats make the lines shimmer, the drop bursts it outwards.

THE SIX PROCEDURAL PIECES (new, in /project1, built by sync_geometry())
  proc_warp       DOMAIN WARPING (Inigo Quilez). Noise evaluated at a point that has
                  been pushed around by more noise: f(p + fbm(p + fbm(p))). Gives smoke,
                  marble, liquid. The kick pushes the warp further.
  proc_attractor  STRANGE ATTRACTOR (Peter de Jong map, catalogued by Paul Bourke):
                  x' = sin(a y) - cos(b x),  y' = sin(c x) - cos(d y).
                  Every pixel is run through the map; it glows where its orbit passes
                  close to a moving point (an "orbit trap"). a, b, c, d drift slowly;
                  the kick nudges them.
  proc_truchet    TRUCHET TILES (Sebastien Truchet, 1704). Each square holds two quarter
                  circles, flipped one way or the other -- the arcs always join into
                  endless winding paths. Tiles re-flip on the beat, so the paths
                  re-route in time with the music.
  proc_contours   CONTOUR LINES of moving noise -- a living topographic map (the same
                  idea as a flow field's streamlines). The kick packs in more lines.
  proc_mandala    SIGNED DISTANCE FIELDS with POLAR REPETITION (Inigo Quilez): one slice
                  of circles and rings, repeated round the centre (8 or 12 slices,
                  alternating every 16 beats). A ring ripples outward on every beat.
  proc_voronoi    VORONOI / WORLEY CELLS: every point belongs to its nearest moving seed;
                  the borders glow. The seeds dance and jump on the kick.

  proc_harmonograph  HARMONOGRAPH (the 19th-century pendulum drawing machine): two
                  decaying swings per axis whose frequency ratios are musical
                  intervals -- 2:3 a fifth, 3:4 a fourth, 4:5 a major third... a new
                  interval every 8 bars. The kick swells it; the hats thicken the line.
  proc_quasicrystal  QUASICRYSTAL: plane waves in 5 (or 7) directions added up -- order
                  that never repeats, with the five-fold symmetry of Penrose tilings.
                  The waves step with the beat; the kick adds more bands.
  proc_life       CELLULAR AUTOMATON (Brian's Brain, Brian Silverman): each cell is on,
                  dying or off; an off cell turns on with exactly two neighbours on. It
                  never settles -- gliders stream across. One generation per 8th note,
                  the kick seeds new cells, a fresh start every 16 bars. (Inside: 'sim'
                  is fed back into itself through 'fb'; 'glsl1' draws it.)
  TRAILS on the finished picture (geo_show > Show page > Trails, 0 = off): every
  frame keeps a fading echo that drifts outward and turns slowly; the kick throws
  it further and a drop bursts it.

  Each one has the same controls as the sacred geometry (Scale, Rotate, Spin, Line
  Width, Glow, Build, Colour A/B on its "Sacred" page) and takes the room's two
  colours. Inside each: a 'shader' DAT (the GLSL code), 'glsl1' (GLSL TOP), 'out1'.

PLAYING WITH THEM IN THE TEXTPORT
  Look at one on its own:            op('/project1/proc_warp').openViewer()
  Make it bigger / turn it:          op('/project1/proc_mandala').par.Spin = 20
  Rebuild all six from scratch:      build_procedural(rebuild=True); sync_geometry()
  What everything is following:      geo_check()      (also saved as td_check.txt)
  Edit the look live: open a piece's 'shader' DAT and change numbers -- e.g. in
  proc_truchet change  7.0  (tiles across) or  0.86  (how many tiles flip each beat).
  The values each shader gets every frame:
     uT  = time, beats, kick, loudness          uS  = scale, rotate, glow, line width
     uCa = colour A + hats                      uCb = colour B + snare
     uX  = build, drop, how bright the sound is
  (When build_hue_hub_td.py is updated, the shader code is refreshed from it -- copy a
  piece you've edited to a new name first if you want to keep your version.)

MORE PROCEDURAL METHODS THAT FIT THIS SET-UP (ideas for next)
  (Feedback trails, a cellular automaton, a harmonograph and the quasicrystal --
  the five-fold cousin of Penrose tilings -- are built now; see above.)
  Curl-noise particles  thousands of GPU particles pushed along the curl of a noise
                     field (Robert Bridson's curl noise): swirling smoke without
                     clumping. TouchDesigner's Particles GPU / POPs can do it.
  L-systems / space colonisation  branching plants and lightning that grow on a build
                     and burst on a drop.
  Fourier epicycles  a drawing traced by chains of rotating circles -- could draw a
                     logo or the outline of a sacred figure on the beat.
  True Penrose tilings (kites and darts, de Bruijn's method) with the rhombs lighting up.
  Diffusion-limited aggregation  coral-like growth that could build over a breakdown.
  Raymarched 3D SDFs  the mandala idea in 3D: Menger sponges, gyroids, infinite
                     repeated temples, flown through on the beat.
